# Interaction & navigation laws (each one shipped a bug before it was a law)

**A row in a `List` uses NATIVE `.swipeActions`** (2026-07-27, #169). Not a preference — a hand-rolled `DragGesture` cannot decline a touch, so it starves the scroll pan on any diagonal flick (see the claim-vs-does law below). UIKit owns the arbitration end to end, and full-swipe-to-commit (#168) comes free. The swipe law is unchanged: **LEADING is curation, TRAILING is destructive**. Don't re-declare the buttons as `.accessibilityActions` — `.swipeActions` publishes its own rotor actions and you'd read every act twice. ⚠️ `.leadingRevealHost(active:)` is STILL required on a pushed screen with leading actions: the app's full-width pop pan refuses simultaneity, so it beats UIKit's cell swipe exactly as it beat the hand-rolled one.

**`SwipeRevealRow` is for rows NOT in a List** — today just the routine-detail rail, in a `ScrollView`, where `.swipeActions` is unavailable until iOS 27 (#277). That rail obeys the same edge law as the catalogs (2026-07-28): **DUPE leads, DELETE trails**. They shared the trailing block until then, which is the only place in the app a non-destructive act sat on the destroy edge — one thumb-width from the delete. Moving it made `RoutineDetailView` a `.leadingRevealHost` (see below), and put a rightward drag on the row whose leading column is the superset RING: safe because the ring is a long PRESS and the pan judges translation, but device-only and independently revertible. Its drag and tap are a UIKit layer (`SwipeGestureLayer`): the pan declines vertical touches in `gestureRecognizerShouldBegin`, and the tap declares `require(toFail:)` on it. Content contract still holds — NO tap affordance in the row body (no Button, no onTapGesture); activation is the component's `onTap`. Companion ordering law (#276): the contentShape + gesture layer attach INSIDE `.offset`, so the probe's bounds ride the translation — attached outside, the hit region keeps the unshifted frame and covers the revealed actions. Full mechanism: docs/DECISIONS.md 2026-07-09 swipe entries + 2026-07-27.

**Leading (swipe-right) reveals need `.leadingRevealHost(active:)` on their screen** (2026-07-17, the equipment quick-add): the full-width back-swipe pan begins at ~10 pt while the reveal drag needs 16, so on a pushed screen the pop wins every rightward drag unless the gate narrows it. The modifier raises `PopGestureGate.leadingRevealHostCount`; while > 0, back-swipe requires a start within the 44 pt edge band — on that screen only. Pass `active: false` the moment the screen pushes something on top (e.g. `active: pushedEquipment == nil`) so the pushed screen keeps full-width pop; a mis-scoped flag kills back-swipe on detail screens, and none of this is CI-visible — device pass required.

**Drilling in from inside a sheet is a `NavigationStack`, never a hand-rolled stage slide** (2026-07-28, Dave: the schedule tray's in-sheet nav "seems kinda janky"). Wrap the sheet's content in a self-contained `NavigationStack` and push with a `NavigationLink` to a screen carrying `.navigationTitle` + `.inline`. ⚠️ **The root bar is VISIBLE as of 2026-08-02** — it IS the sheet's header, declared by `sheetChrome` (design-grammar). The `.toolbar(.hidden, for: .navigationBar)` this rule used to require existed only to keep the app's hand-drawn `SheetHeader` in frame; leaving it on now hides the title and the sheet's only way out. Precedents: `ScheduleTray`'s schedule-mode row, `RevealSurface`'s Voice-cues row, `NavigationSelectRow` (single-select) and `SheetPickList` (multi-select, searchable). ⚠️ The ZStack + `.move` transition idiom is RETIRED — `SwapInSheet` and `ScheduleRoutineTray` were the last two and converted 2026-07-28. It fails for STRUCTURAL reasons, not tunable ones: no interactive back-swipe; both stages exist mid-transition at different intrinsic heights so the sheet resolves to the taller one and settles; the header sits outside the ZStack so the title hard-swaps while the content slides; `.clipped()` fights the sheet's corner radius; and VoiceOver gets a bare chevron instead of a system Back carrying the origin's title. Reduce Motion and RTL come free from the stack and have to be hand-held otherwise. A linear WIZARD (`GitHubSyncTray`'s three steps with Continue/Back) is a different shape — a stepper, not navigation — and may keep the slide. Presentation-level modifiers (`.presentationBackground`, `.interactiveDismissDisabled`, detents) go OUTSIDE the `NavigationStack`, or they address the root screen instead of the sheet.


**Two companion rules for that stack** (2026-07-28, both learned converting the last two trays). ⚠️ **The HOST owns the stack, never the embedded content** — `ScheduleEditor` used to bring its own so it would work in either embed context, which nested one inside a stack the moment its host became one; the host owns the header, so it owns the stack, and the embedded view is plain content. ⚠️ **A sheet whose detent must GROW on push binds `NavigationStack(path:)` and drives the detent off `path.count`**, never a stage flag: depth also changes on the back SWIPE and on a `navigationDestination` pop, which a flag set by a button never sees. (`SwapInSheet` was the worked example while its root was two keys tall; build 159 outgrew the compact detent and then DELETED the tray with the play key — the band's Train key took the scratch start — so the law currently has no live consumer. It stands for the next compact sheet that pushes a list. ⚠️ The companion lesson from that retirement: a ScaledMetric compact detent was also the sheet's Dynamic Type protection — replace one with a fixed detent and the content below the fold needs a ScrollView, or AX sizes strand keys under an unscrollable edge.)

**Two ways to open the SAME sheet is ONE `.sheet(item:)`, never two `.sheet(isPresented:)`** (2026-08-02, #509 review). A view presents one sheet at a time, so a second request arriving while the first is up is silently dropped — AND it latches its flag `true`, since only that sheet's own dismissal resets it, after which every later request is a no-op assignment and the route is dead until relaunch. `RootTabView` had exactly this: `showGitHubConnect` (the post-install Setup-URL bounce) beside `showSyncTray` (Today's broken-sync advisory), and the collision is the LIKELY path, not a corner — re-installing the Sync App is how a broken sync gets repaired, `openInstall()` leaves the app on purpose so the bounce can come back, and the bounce lands while the tray opened from Today is still on screen. As an item, a second request REPLACES the first. ⚠️ Where two genuinely different sheets must share a view, `RevealSurface`'s pending-queue is the pattern (`activeTray` is nil before `activePush` is set, so they never present in the same frame). ⚠️ And a tray with a `didInit`-guarded `onAppear` will NOT re-seat itself for a request that arrives while it is already open — decide what the live instance should do, or the request reaches a screen that ignores it.

**A `ToolbarItem` that can be EMPTY must not be emitted: the condition goes around the ITEM, never inside it** (2026-08-03, build 177 — Dave's screenshot of Barbell). iOS 26 draws a Liquid Glass container around whatever a bar item holds, and an item holding nothing is a fully-formed, perfectly round, completely BLANK button. SwiftUI cannot prune it for you: a `ToolbarItem` wrapping an `if` is still an item, so guarding the CONTENTS only empties the glass, it does not remove it. ⚠️ The blast radius comes from shared chrome, which is why this is a law and not a footnote — `pushedScreenChrome` emitted its trailing item unconditionally and wrapped the caller's views in an `HStack`, so ONE mistake reached four screens: the session record, template detail and the presented catalog pass no trailing keys at all, and equipment detail's rename + `…` both sit behind `!equipment.isBuiltIn`, so every built-in piece in the Kit grew a blank key. ⚠️ **The fix is TYPE-level, and that is the part to carry**: a chrome modifier takes trailing content as **`ToolbarContent`** (`@ToolbarContentBuilder`), never as a `View`. Only then does a call site's `if` omit the ITEM — a `View` builder can only ever hand back emptiness for someone else to wrap. `sheetChrome` had it right from the start (`if let cancel` / `if let confirm`); `pushedScreenChrome` now matches, with a no-trailing overload for the screens that have no keys. ⚠️ Invisible to CI: XCUITest reaches controls through accessibility and a blank bar item has no name to assert on, so this class is device-pass only.

**A permission ask that fires off a live edit is INLINE, never a sheet** (2026-08-02, #509 Q20-B — the calendar offer). Three reasons, all structural: the edit persists on every keystroke or chip tap, so a modal lands on the FIRST one and interrupts the rest of the edit; the editor is itself presented modally by both its hosts, so the primer would be a sheet over a sheet; and resolving it on the editor's way out is dismiss-then-present in one transaction, the documented presentation-drop class (`HealthStartPrimer` carries that law). An offer that sits in the form and waits costs nothing and asks nothing. ⚠️ Gate it on "would the OS still prompt" (`EKEventStore.authorizationStatus == .notDetermined`, or the equivalent), not just on "have we asked" — a key that does nothing when tapped is worse than no offer. And keep the app's OWN one-shot memory beside it: the system status answers "did iOS decide", never "did PlusPlus already offer", and those come apart the moment someone taps Not now.

**A multi-select is a list, not a `Menu`** (2026-07-28). A SwiftUI `Menu` can't be searched, closes on every pick (so adding four things means opening it four times), and shows no selection state, which forces removal to live somewhere else. Picked items render as chips in the form; one `+ Add` key pushes `SheetPickList`, where selection toggles in place. Long lists get a search field and one flat run; a list you can see all of at once gets grouping and no field.

**Value navigation destinations register at stack roots, never on pushed screens** (#262). And **a pushed screen that appends to the path must itself be a path entry** (#291): value appends BENEATH a live `isPresented:`/item destination replace it and break back-pop; boolean destinations ON TOP of value pushes are fine. `path.append` is not idempotent — root-only affordances guard on `path.isEmpty`; template-row pushes carry a one-shot flag reset in `onAppear`.

**Routine-family presentation/navigation keys on the model's `uuid`, never `persistentModelID`** (2026-07-14, the tray-flicker decoupling): `persistentModelID` swaps temporary→permanent at a fresh model's first save and re-keys an open sheet/push (the flicker). Push/present a small value type carrying the `uuid` (`ModelRefs.swift`: `RoutineRef`, `IdentifiedUUID`) and resolve the model in the destination via `ModelContext.routine(uuid:)` (a FETCH, so a just-created routine resolves with no `@Query` lag). This covers EVERY push, `NavigationLink(value:)` included — not just `path.append` (three `NavigationLink(value: routine)` sites in TodayView's today/upcoming/carried cards were missed in the first decoupling pass and became dead taps: pushing a value with NO registered `.navigationDestination(for:)` is a **silent runtime no-op, not a compile error**, and `ui-test` is skipped on branch builds, so only an on-device tap surfaced it — a device pass is mandatory after any routine-nav change). `Routine`/`ExerciseGroup`/`RoutineExercise` `uuid` is `UUID?` (optional for a lightweight migration) but effectively always non-nil (init default + launch backfill), so `.map`/`if let` at the few call sites. Internal animation/list identity (the zoom-transition pairing, `ForEach` in browse lists, swipe-open state) MAY stay on `persistentModelID` — it's not a presentation that flickers, and the door-saves already make it safe.

**Screens that commit edits on exit must also commit in `onDisappear`** — the full-width swipe-back pops entirely in UIKit, so SwiftUI `onBack` closures never run. Idempotent, guarded against the deleted-model race.

**Long-press row gestures use `.simultaneousGesture`** — `.gesture(LongPressGesture().sequenced(DragGesture()))` starves the ScrollView pan (#99). The rail's gestures live on a UIKit `UILongPressGestureRecognizer` attached to the enclosing UIScrollView for exactly this reason; `scrollDisabled` only during an active drag.

**Guarding what a gesture DOES is not guarding what it CLAIMS** (2026-07-27, #169 — the intermittent dead scroll). A `DragGesture` whose handler `return`s early on the wrong direction still *recognized*, and recognition is what starves the scroll pan — the drawer sat still and so did the list. Two rules for any drag layered over scrollable content: attach it with **`.simultaneousGesture`, never `.gesture`**, and **latch the direction verdict on the sequence's first event** in `@GestureState` rather than re-asking per event (a re-asked guard lets a scroll that curves sideways hand over the touch mid-flick). `RevealContainer`'s 16 pt swipe-to-open strip is the worked example: it is the topmost layer over every tab root and lies exactly on each row's leading margin, so a `.gesture` there is a standing dead column. ⚠️ Invisible to CI — XCUITest dispatches through accessibility and bypasses gesture overlays, so every change in this class needs a device pass.

**Not every dead scroll is a gesture conflict** (2026-07-27). Before hunting recognizers, check whether the main thread is simply BLOCKED: a stall delivers the touch sequence late and collapsed, so the flick does nothing and the user retries — which reads exactly like a hit-testing dead zone but is position-independent. The tell is that it tracks a *moment* (arriving on a tab, finishing a save) rather than a *place*. `GitHubSyncCoordinator.requestSync` is the known instance — `.syncsProgramOnClose()` on every tab root, +1500 ms, then an archive rebuild. ⚠️ Its encode and base read moved OFF the main actor (#509, b15) and it is still named here on purpose: `ModelContext` isn't `Sendable`, so the export fetch and the per-session `.externalStorage` route reads stay, and those are the bigger half. Reduced, not fixed. If the symptom is mid-screen or moves around, suspect a stall first; if it is reproducibly in one region, suspect a claim.

**A scrolling surface that holds a text field MUST declare `.scrollDismissesKeyboard`** (2026-07-31, #489/#490 — the exercise editor and routine settings both shipped with NO way to put the keyboard away). A plain SwiftUI `ScrollView` does not dismiss on scroll by default, and tapping a `Button` does not resign first responder on iOS, so a form that declares nothing strands the keyboard until the screen closes. Scroll dismissal is the LOAD-BEARING exit; `.keyboardGround(clearing:)` (`Views/Components/KeyboardGround.swift`, which carries the full reasoning) is always the SECOND one. Invisible to CI: XCUITest taps dispatch through accessibility and bypass this layer, so every change here needs a device pass.

**Four traps in that law, each paid for once.** ⚠️ The ground is a layer BEHIND the content, never an `.onTapGesture` on the stack — an ancestor gesture races the field's own focus tap, and losing that race blinks the keyboard shut on the way IN, which is worse than no dismissal. ⚠️ Its catchment is thinner than "the gaps": labels, captions, each field's filled chrome (padding ring included) and any card background take the tap first, leaving the side margins and inter-section bands. ⚠️ **No header in the app can carry a ground any more** (2026-08-02): `pushedScreenChrome` and `sheetChrome` are both the SYSTEM navigation bar, and a platform bar is not a view of ours. The rule used to say a sibling header needs its OWN ground and `ExerciseEditorView`'s had one — ~90 pt of the sheet's most conspicuous blank space, directly above the field holding the keyboard. That exit is GONE, which is what makes `.scrollDismissesKeyboard` and a ground on the form's own CONTENT the whole of the answer now. ⚠️ `.scrollDismissesKeyboard` is an ENVIRONMENT value and reaches a `TextField(axis: .vertical)`'s own internal scroll — give multi-line fields `.scrollDismissesKeyboard(.never)` or a drag inside one drops the keyboard mid-edit. And clear focus on every route that opens something OVER the form (a push, a wheel, a tray, a confirm) or hands off to another presentation: `save()` was the miss in #489's first pass, and it is the exit that presents the next sheet.

**`opacity(0)` does NOT remove a view from hit testing** — hidden layers need `allowsHitTesting(false)`.

**And the converse: a `.frame()` does NOT add hit testing** (2026-08-02, build 171 — the search dock's ✕ "clicks whatever's behind it, as if it were pointer-events: none"). A `.frame(width:height:)` around an `Image` is LAYOUT SPACE; SwiftUI hit tests the CONTENT, so a `Button` whose label is a glyph in a big frame is only tappable on the glyph's own strokes. ⚠️ **The thing that has always saved `HeaderIconButton` is its opaque `.background(_:in:)`** — a real background VIEW fills the frame and is hit-testable, so nobody had to think about it. Swap that ground for a rendering EFFECT (`.glassEffect`, and by the same logic `.blur`, `.shadow`, a `Canvas` overlay) and the hit target silently collapses to the glyph. Fix is `.contentShape(...)` as the OUTERMOST modifier, so it defines the finished view's hit region rather than one that a later wrapper can re-compose away. ⚠️ The failure is worse than a dead key, because a near-miss lands on whatever is behind: on the dock that was the list row underneath, or sideways onto the in-field clear key — where clearing the text and keeping focus reads as "the ✕ does nothing", a completely different-looking bug with the same cause. ⚠️ XCUITest cannot see any of it: taps dispatch through accessibility, which uses the accessibility frame, not the hit region. Device pass, always.

**One-shot deferred UI beats re-check preconditions at fire time** (StartFlashButton's deferred fire, the lingering +1 finish): cancel on disappear, check `isDeleted`/session state before acting.

**A long modifier chain is ONE expression, and the type-checker has a budget for it** (2026-08-02, CI red on #509; `TodayView.committedHistory` learned it first). `RootTabView.appContent` carried ~230 lines of `.onReceive`/`.onOpenURL`/`.sheet`/`.alert` and went over the moment a `.sheet(isPresented:)` pair became one `.sheet(item:)` with a ternary in its builder: **"the compiler is unable to type-check this expression in reasonable time"**, which fails the build, not just the file. ⚠️ Invisible to `swiftc -parse` — the budget is a TYPE-checking limit and parse-only has no SDK to type-check against (testing.md), so a Linux session gets a clean parse and a red `test` job. The fix is a `some View` boundary, never a smaller change to the modifier that tipped it: split the chain into a second computed property (`routedAppContent` wraps `appContent`) and add below the split from then on. Same law for a big `@ViewBuilder` stack — extract, don't inline one more `Section`.

**Shared controls live in `Views/Components/`** once they appear in a second view — never redefined across screens.
