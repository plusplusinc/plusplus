What separates them

Height is the whole trade

The numbers on each card are measured, not estimated: where the first exercise row starts, from the top of the scroll. The spread between the tallest (1, at 215 pt) and the shortest (6, at 125 pt) is 90 pt, which on this list is about one and a half exercise rows.

It only bites on arrival. Everything scrolls now, so a tall header costs you the first screenful and nothing after it — which is an argument for spending the height on legibility rather than hoarding it.

⚠ the one real conflict

Direction 4 cannot pin the columns

If the rail runs continuously from the metadata down into the exercises, a pinned band sitting between them cuts the spine it is meant to be part of. So direction 4 pins nothing, and target / prev scroll away with everything else.

That is a genuine loss against what you asked for last round, and it is the price of the idea rather than a detail to fix. Worth seeing before deciding it is too expensive.

Direction 6 argues with a settled call

Every other direction keeps four separate doors. This one keeps a single summary that opens the settings sheet, which is close to where the screen started before this round.

It earns its place here because it is the honest maximum for list height, and because seeing it makes the cost of the other five legible. It is not a recommendation.

Amber has to survive the form change

Whatever the shape, a piece of equipment the active kit lacks has to read as advisory and stay tappable. In a table that is a value in notesInk; in prose it is a clause; in the rail it is a tick node.

The chip is the only form where the existing law already covers it. The rest need a decision about what "interactive amber wears the control shape" means when there is no chip.

Notes, removed

Gone from this region in all seven. Nothing here has a place for a free-text field, and the settings sheet already holds one.

Everything else is unchanged
