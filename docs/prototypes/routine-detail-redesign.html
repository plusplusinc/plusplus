What separates them

Why 1a is loaded

The header's values end at exactly the x where the list's target column ends. Measured, not eyeballed: 80 pt in from the screen edge, which is the rail's right padding plus the prev cell plus the gap between them.

That one line of arithmetic is what turns a header and a list into a single table. It also let the hairlines go, since a shared column organises rows better than rules between them, and folding transition into the rest row took four rows to three. Those two together are the 44 pt.

⚠ constrains direction 3

SwiftUI cannot box a token inside wrapping text

The repo already knows this one: KitTag works only with the kit name last, because a padded rounded tag cannot sit inside a wrapping Text run. Anywhere it cannot go, the name takes its noun instead.

So direction 3's tokens can never be chips. What they can be is inline links: coloured, underlined, tapped through a custom URL scheme and openURL, which composes fine with wrapping. That is 3a, and it is the version of this idea that is actually buildable. 3b sidesteps the question by taking the taps out of the prose.

What 5a fixes

PT A needs two things, so the 2×2's kit cell looked fine. Give a routine a barbell, a rack, a bench and bands and that cell truncates while three short cells beside it sit half empty.

Three equal cells for the three settings, and a full-width row for the kit, sizes each cell to what it actually holds. Costs 4 pt.

Amber still needs a ruling

The law says interactive amber wears the control shape. In 1a and 5a it is a value in notesInk with a chevron; in 3a it is an underlined token. Neither is a chip.

The reading that holds: the row is the control and wears the shape, and amber is the ink inside it. That keeps flag-don't-hide without inventing a second amber treatment. Worth confirming before it gets written down as a law.
