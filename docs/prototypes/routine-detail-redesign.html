Rebuilt on the original anatomy: real mono caps labels, a hairline under every row, four separate rows, values hard right. The number on each card is the measured worst-case gap from the end of a label to the start of its value; the reference sits at 267 pt. H is loaded — your combination, with two switches under the list for the parts of it that had no obvious answer.

H · saying which pause is which

Combining the two rows is the easy half. Making one row say 45s between sets, 15s between exercises without a sentence is the hard half. Three mechanisms, and the sheet carries the full explanation in all of them: tap the row to read it.

1 reads rest · transition against 45s · 15s and lets position do the work, which mono columns are good at and which costs no height. 2 spells it out in two lines with the noun beside each number, unambiguous but twice as tall and the only one that survives a screenshot with no context. 3 shows 45s · 15s under a single pauses label and leaves the disambiguation entirely to the sheet, which is the smallest header and the biggest bet on people tapping.

H · the kit row, without the middle dot

The · is doing list-separator duty between two equipment names, and at mono sizes it floats at mid-height with nothing to sit on, so it reads as debris rather than punctuation. Four ways to drop it.

i · data tags removes the separator instead of replacing it: each name gets CardTagCapsule's soft r6 fill, which is already how the app draws routine gear pills, and already what the muscle groups on the left of this same header are wearing. The tag boundary is the separator. It also gives the amber a shape to live in rather than a lone coloured word, and it scales to a kit of five without the row turning into a chain of dots. Note the tags are sentence-case and not mono while the rest of the column is: that is the data-tag law, not an oversight.

ii · one per line matches the pauses row directly above it, so both multi-value rows read the same way. Honest and clear, but a kit of five costs five lines. iii · comma is the smallest possible change and sits on the baseline where the dot did not, though a comma inside a mono data row reads as prose. iv · just a gap is the minimal answer and breaks the moment a piece of equipment has two words in its name, which most of them do.

H · making the kit tags look like doors

The surface law already answers this: shape says what taps. A card data tag is soft r6 with no stroke and is inert; anything interactive wears the control shape, r11 and stroked, like every other key in the app. The muscle groups stay inert in all four of these, which is the whole point — the contrast is what makes the shape mean anything.

i is the law applied literally: r11 and stroked, so the kit reads as two keys and the muscle groups read as two facts, with nothing else needing to change. ii adds a chevron inside each tag, which is more explicit but re-introduces exactly the asymmetry you flagged in the first screenshot if it ever ends up on only some of them. iii keeps the soft ground and adds only the stroke, a half-step that reads as a tag that happens to tap; it stays visually closer to the muscle groups than i does.

iv is the other honest answer, and it is not a cop-out: make the tags genuinely inert and let the row be the single door, chevron and all, exactly like schedule and pauses. Then looking identical to the muscle groups is correct, because neither taps. It costs the per-piece resolve sheet, which is the thing amber is for.

⚠️ In i, ii and iii the row loses its chevron, because a row-level door plus per-tag doors would be nested tap targets. That is deliberate, and it does mean the kit row stops matching the other two rows' trailing chevron.

H · what else rides the estimate column

You asked what could join it. Everything here is derivable from data the app already holds, and each answers a different question you might have while looking at this screen.

b · done by 1:18 is the estimate finished for you. "~30 min" still asks you to do arithmetic against a clock you have to go and look at; a finish time is the answer to the actual question, which is whether this fits before the next thing. It is the only addition here that makes the estimate more useful rather than adding a second fact beside it.

c · last tue, 7 days ago pairs with the prev column below, so the whole screen reads as "here is what you did and when". d · muscle groups replaces the vaguer "Upper" with what the exercises actually cover, drawn from the multi-select that already exists on each one. a is the control: if the column looks unbalanced, that is worth knowing before filling it.

Two tag families, one header

With the kit as tags and the muscle groups already as tags, the header now carries the same soft r6 pill in two columns for two different kinds of thing. On the render they separate fine, because the KIT label and the column gap do the work.

It is still the one thing worth a real look. If they blur, the muscle groups are the ones to change, not the kit: gear pills wearing CardTagCapsule is the older, more established use.

the new one

H does two things at once, and they compound

Moving the estimate left and merging rest into transition are separate ideas, but they pull the same way. Three rows instead of four shortens the table; a 104 pt column on the left shortens it again. Travel lands at 121 against the reference's 267, and the first exercise arrives at 161 pt against 215 — the shortest header of anything built so far, without narrowing the table into a stub.

It also fixes something the four-row version had: TRANSITION was the longest label in the block and set the left edge for everything. With it merged, no label is unusually long and the column reads even.

correction

Right-shifting was a finding about 1a, not about this table

Last round C came in at 61 pt and I called it the answer. That held because 1a pulled its value column 60 pt in from the edge, so moving the table right closed a gap that was partly artificial.

On the original, values right-align to the column edge whichever side the table sits on, so C lands at 135 — no better than B and worse than E. The general claim was wrong. What survives is narrower: where the label starts matters more than how wide the table is, which is why every split here beats the reference and why the two-column split beats them all.

Why F is the pick

It is the only option that changes nothing you liked. Four rows, real labels, full width, values hard right — all intact. The rule stops sitting under the row and runs across it instead, from the end of the label to the value.

The distance is still 267 pt. That is the point: an index leader does not shorten the trip, it removes the cost of making it, because the eye tracks a line instead of jumping a void. If the gap still reads as too far with a leader in it, then the problem was the width after all and E is the answer.

Measured, worst case

Reference 267 · F 267 (led) · A 141 · B 125 · C 135 · G 135 · D 157 · E 94.

Worst case, not average: the widest gap in the block, always the row with the shortest label. KIT against a right-aligned value is what sets it in most of these.

E is the shortest header too

Two columns of two rows lands the first exercise at 155 pt against the reference's 215. It is both the shortest travel and the most list, which is a strong combination for something that invents nothing new.

What it costs is a single reading order. Four rows down a column is one path; a 2×2 is two, and nothing on the screen says which to take first.

B is the quiet contender

Lifting the kit out is the only change that fixes a second problem at the same time: the kit is the one row whose value length actually varies, so it is the row that will truncate first as a routine's equipment grows.

It also leaves three rows of near-equal label length, which is what makes its 125 respectable without any typographic help.

Unchanged underneath

Names wrap, the node centres on the first line, the ledger prints target beside prev with no delta, the columns pin, Start plays the ignition. The header itself pins nothing in any of these, so these rows scroll away with the title.
